Food photos for Instagram vs. for your menu

A photo that works on your menu might look flat on Instagram. A great Instagram photo might look out of place on your menu. They're doing different jobs.

You don't need two shoots, though. You just need to plan one shoot with both in mind.

What makes a good menu photo

A menu photo has one job. Show someone exactly what they're going to get.

  • Consistent. Same angle, same light, same background across every dish. When someone scrolls your online menu, the photos should look like they belong together.
  • Clean. The dish is the whole story. No hands, no clutter, no props competing with the food.
  • Honest. Real portion, real plate. If the photo promises more than the plate delivers, people notice.
  • Horizontal or square. Most websites, printed menus and delivery apps use those shapes.

What makes a good Instagram food photo

Instagram is the opposite. People scroll past things that look the same.

  • Variety. Close-ups, overheads, a full table, a single bite. Different backgrounds and colors from post to post.
  • Hands and action. Someone dipping a chip, pulling a slice, pouring a drink. It makes a photo feel alive.
  • Vertical. 4:5 fills the most screen in the feed. Stories and Reels covers are 9:16.
  • A little mess is fine. Crumbs, a drip of sauce, a napkin. It looks like somebody's about to eat it.

Can one food shoot cover both?

Yes, if we plan it that way. This is how I usually do it.

Each dish gets its clean, matched menu shot first while it's perfect. Then I get a vertical or two for social. A closer crop, a different angle, maybe a hand coming in. Same plate, a couple of extra minutes.

I'll also leave a bit of room around the food in some shots so they can be cropped either way. A horizontal with space on the sides can become a 4:5 vertical. You can't go the other way.

If you tell me up front roughly how many photos you want for each, I'll make sure the numbers come out right.

What about the delivery apps?

The delivery apps sit closer to the menu side. Food big in the frame, centered, simple background. The photos show up small, so a busy scene turns into mush on a phone. Clean, bright, straight-on or overhead works best.

And your website?

Your website can take a bit of both. The menu page wants the clean, matched shots. The homepage can use something with more going on, like a full table or a drink being poured. Wide horizontals work best up top, since most site headers are wide and short.

How often you need new photos

Menu photos last as long as the menu does. A good set can carry you until the next big change.
